# Review of the original brief: where it went against the style

As a value-and-momentum practitioner, these are the points I would push back on. Each one is addressed in the style plans.

| # | Your suggestion | Issue | Recommended change |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **50 DMA / 200 DMA in Value analysis** | Moving averages are *price-trend* (technical) tools. Classic value investing deliberately ignores price trend: it buys when price is *below* intrinsic value, which often means *below* the 200 DMA. Scoring DMA inside Value would penalise the very stocks value investing is looking for. | Keep DMAs **out of the Value score**. Use them only as an **Entry-Timing Overlay** in the Value report ("cheap *and* the trend has stopped falling", which guards against catching a falling knife). The overlay decides *how* to buy (staggered or lump sum), not *whether* to buy. |
| 2 | **P/E as a core value metric** | An absolute P/E is misleading on its own. (a) Indian markets structurally trade at higher multiples than the US, so US rules of thumb (P/E < 15) screen out nearly all Indian quality companies. (b) Cyclicals (metals, cement, sugar) look *cheapest at the top of the cycle*. (c) For banks and NBFCs, P/E matters less than **P/B or P/ABV**. (d) Loss-making companies have no meaningful P/E. | Use P/E **relative to the stock's own 5-year and 10-year median** and **relative to its sector median**. Use P/B for financials and normalised (cycle-average) earnings for cyclicals. |
| 3 | **PEG ratio** | PEG depends heavily on *which growth number* you use. Forward consensus estimates are thin or missing for most Indian small caps, and PEG is meaningless for negative-growth, low-growth, cyclical or high-dividend companies. | Compute PEG on **trailing 3-year EPS CAGR** (with forward growth as an optional second view). Mark it "N/A" rather than force it when growth is ≤ 0 or above 50% (base effect). Give it a **modest weight**. |
| 4 | **"50 DMA crossing 200 DMA" and "200 DMA crossing 50 DMA"** | These are the same pair of lines, so it's one event seen from two sides. The standard terms are **Golden Cross** (50 crosses *above* 200, bullish) and **Death Cross** (50 crosses *below* 200, bearish). Both are **lagging**: by the time they fire, much of the move has usually happened. | Keep both, but treat them as **confirmation signals, not triggers**. Put leading signals ahead of them: relative-strength line highs, 52-week-high breakouts on volume, and Stage 1→2 transitions (plan 02). |
| 5 | **Momentum = performance vs benchmark** | This is correct, and it's the heart of momentum. Two refinements: (a) the benchmark must match the cap (Nifty 50 is the wrong yardstick for a small cap); (b) raw returns reward volatile stocks, so the industry standard (including NSE's Nifty200 Momentum 30 methodology) uses **volatility-adjusted** 6-month and 12-month returns. | Benchmark by cap: **Nifty 100 / Nifty Midcap 150 / Nifty Smallcap 250**, plus the stock's **sector index**. Add **12-1 month momentum** (skip the latest month to avoid short-term reversal) and a volatility-adjusted score. |
| 6 | **Monthly cadence for momentum** | Monthly suits Value and momentum *ranking*, but momentum *signals* such as crosses and breakdowns decay within days. | Monthly run for full scoring and reports, plus a **daily end-of-day watcher** for momentum state changes (you asked for this as well). |
| 7 | **One BUY/SELL answer from both styles** | Value and momentum are **negatively correlated** by design: value buys what has fallen, momentum buys what is rising. Averaging the two scores produces mushy "HOLD"s and hides the useful information. | Score them **separately** and combine with a **2-D decision matrix** (section 5). A disagreement between them is itself a signal (for example, "cheap but still falling: WATCH"). |
| 8 | **Binary BUY / SELL** | Too coarse for monthly review, and you'd flip-flop near thresholds. | Use a graded verdict, a **confidence level** (based on data completeness) and **hysteresis**: a verdict changes only if the score moves more than 5 points past the threshold. |
